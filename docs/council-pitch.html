Bluff Run
A proposal for how 1 v 100 should work

One seat. A council of Jevs.

Every stage is heads-up. The seat across from you is N Jevs, each with its own temperament, each asked the same question. The seat does what the vote says. At stage 1 it is one Jev. At stage 8 it is a hundred.

How it works today

A tournament you mostly can't see

At stage 100 you sit with eight. The other 92 play each other on the server. You beat your table, then the survivors come back, then it's a final. Correct poker structure, but "beating 100" is really beating eight three times, and most of the field is invisible.

The council

You against all of them, every hand

Each council member votes fold, call or raise from the same table state. The seat takes the majority and sizes raises as the average of the raisers. You see the vote fill in before the seat moves. It is literally you versus N, and the vote becomes the signature visual of the game.

Where the differences come from

Three layers, seeded per member

Jev is a decision model: the same table in, the same probabilities out. A hundred identical calls would be one call. So each member gets a brief, and the shape of its bot tells you what kind it is.

Archetype (real poker types, each with a known exploit)

Quirk (one situational habit)

River shutdownFires flop and turn, gives up on the river when called twice.
Blind stealerRaises from the button with almost anything.
TrapperChecks its monsters and lets you bet into them.
Scared moneyFolds to big bets regardless of odds.
Never folds an aceExactly that.
TiltAfter losing a big pot, plays loose for three hands.

Tell (something you can actually read)

TimingTakes longer to vote when it's strong. A long deliberation means something.
Wide eyesThe bot's eyes widen a beat before a raise.
The blinkBlinks before it folds.
NoneMost members have no tell. Which ones do is hidden until the run ends.

Tells are the part that teaches poker. Readers who notice can exploit them; the reveal confirms what they saw.

Why difficulty rises for a real reason

After the run

The council you faced

When a run ends, every member you played is revealed: name, archetype, quirk, tell, how often it voted against the majority, and its best read, the hand where its minority vote would have been right. In the hand review each decision shows the split, and hovering a bar names who voted for it.

Seeded councils you can share

The run seed is on the result and in the share link, so a friend sits down against exactly the same hundred. "Beat my run" becomes "beat my council".

Table talk without a text model

Jev can't write, but it can choose. After a big pot, one extra question (needle, respect, quiet) maps to a few short lines per archetype. Cheap, and the council gets a voice.
